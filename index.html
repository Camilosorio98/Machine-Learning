Context

Informality in Colombia

According to DANE, approximately 63% of Colombian workers are employed in the informal sector. Mid-sized cities such as Fusagasugá, Pasto, and Montería show even higher rates, with limited social security coverage and access to formal credit.

📍 Study Area

Colombian cities with open data available on the datos.gov.co portal and DANE labor market records.

🎓 Stakeholders

Municipal governments, Chambers of Commerce, MinCIT, economic policy researchers, and the academic project team.

Phase 03 · CRISP-ML

Machine Learning Model

Application of unsupervised learning to discover latent patterns of commercial informality without the need for predefined labels.

Unsupervised Learning

K-Means Clustering · Pattern Detection

K-Means groups commercial records into k clusters based on similarity in economic variables. Each cluster represents a trader profile, automatically identifying high-informality segments without the need for pre-labeled data.

🔍
Why K-Means? No labels required. Efficiently identifies hidden structures in multidimensional economic data.
📐
Selecting k Elbow Method and Silhouette Coefficient to determine the optimal number of clusters.
⚡
Preprocessing Normalization with StandardScaler and dimensionality reduction with PCA for result visualization.
📈
Evaluation Metrics Silhouette Score, Davies-Bouldin Index, and cohesion/separation analysis between clusters.
🗺️
Visualization Heat maps by municipality, 2D/3D scatter plots, and dashboards with cluster profiles.
🛠️
Tech Stack Python · scikit-learn · pandas · matplotlib · seaborn · Jupyter Notebook · GitHub

✅ Expected Outcome

Identify between 3 and 5 trader profiles with varying degrees of informality, generating differentiated public policy recommendations by segment and municipality.

⚠️ Limitations

Sensitivity to outliers, the need to scale data, and the number of clusters must be defined a priori. Quality depends directly on the availability and completeness of open data.
