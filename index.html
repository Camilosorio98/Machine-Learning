Phase 01 · CRISP-ML

Business and Data Understanding

Colombia has one of the highest informality rates in Latin America. According to DANE's GEIH (2024), 55.8% of all employed workers operate informally — and the rate jumps to 84.7% inside micro-businesses, which employ 62% of the country's workforce. This project uses open data to make that problem measurable and actionable.

🎯

Business Objectives

  • ◆ Apply K-Means clustering to the DANE EMICRON dataset to automatically segment Colombian micro-businesses into distinct informality profiles.
  • ◆ Give local governments and chambers of commerce a data-driven tool to prioritize formalization efforts — moving beyond the blunt national average (55.8%) toward actionable, city-level intelligence.
  • ◆ Identify which segments are closest to formalization ("gateway clusters") so that MinCIT and DNP can design targeted incentive programs with the highest return on public investment.
  • ◆ Produce a reproducible pipeline that can be re-run each year as new EMICRON data is released, enabling longitudinal tracking of informality cluster evolution across Colombia.
❓

Key Questions

The model must answer:

  • ◆ Which combination of variables — income level, RUT registration, ARL contribution, location type, and CIIU sector — most strongly predicts a business's degree of informality?
  • ◆ Which cities and departments show the highest concentration of deeply informal clusters? Are Caribbean coast cities (Sincelejo 70%, Riohacha 63.9%) systematically different from Andean cities (Bogotá 32.9%)?
  • ◆ Can we identify a "gateway to formality" cluster — businesses that are technically informal but already have partial compliance (e.g., have RUT but no Cámara de Comercio registration)?
  • ◆ Does the economic sector (CIIU) determine informality level independently of city? For example, are street-food vendors structurally more informal than home-based manufacturing businesses?
  • ◆ How stable are the detected clusters across annual EMICRON releases (2022 → 2023 → 2024)? Is informality decreasing within the same segments, or are clusters shifting composition?
📊

Data Assessment

  • ◆ Primary source: EMICRON 2023 (DANE) — over 12,000 downloads on microdatos.dane.gov.co. Covers micro-businesses with up to 9 employees across 24 departments and Bogotá D.C.
  • ◆ The dataset includes 7 structured modules: economic activity (CIIU), entrepreneurship history, location type, workforce profile, business formalization, ICT usage, and financial inclusion.
  • ◆ Complementary source: GEIH (Gran Encuesta Integrada de Hogares) — used to validate city-level informality rates and enrich the model with regional labor market context.
  • ◆ Data quality note: the "Ventas mes anterior" (monthly sales) field had a 7% imputation rate in Q4 2024 — handled via median imputation by sector and city to avoid bias in income-based features.
⚖️

Feasibility Analysis

  • ◆ Technically viable: EMICRON is publicly available, annually updated, and well-documented with a DDI/XML data dictionary — all dependencies are open-source Python libraries.
  • ◆ Privacy constraint: the dataset has been anonymized by DANE — direct and pseudo-identifiers are removed, so no individual business can be re-identified. This limits sub-municipal spatial analysis.
  • ◆ Coverage gap: EMICRON does not cover 8 low-density departments (Arauca, Casanare, Vichada, Guainía, Guaviare, Vaupés, Putumayo, Amazonas) — results are representative of urban and semi-urban Colombia only.
  • ◆ Ethical consideration: cluster labels must be framed as structural policy targets — not as judgments on individual businesses. Findings should inform incentives, not penalties.

Real Problem · DANE Data

Why This Problem Matters

Cities with the highest informality rates (GEIH, 2023) are Sincelejo (70%), Riohacha (63.9%), and Valledupar (63.3%). The lowest are Bogotá (32.9%), Manizales (34.2%), and Medellín (37%). This 37-point spread shows that informality is highly localized — exactly the kind of pattern ML clustering can detect and quantify. Meanwhile, 93.6% of micro-business owners made no contributions to occupational risk insurance (ARL) in 2024, leaving millions of workers completely unprotected.

📍 Cities in Scope

The EMICRON dataset covers 24 departments + Bogotá D.C., with city-level breakdowns available for the 23 main metropolitan areas tracked by the GEIH — including Sincelejo, Barranquilla, Cali, Medellín, and Bogotá.

🎓 Stakeholders

Municipal governments, MinCIT, Chambers of Commerce, DNP (Departamento Nacional de Planeación), and academic researchers in labor economics and public policy.

📉
Downward trend — but slow National informality fell from 58.0% (2022) to 55.8% (2024) — a 2.2 p.p. drop in two years (DANE, GEIH).
🏪
Micro-businesses are the epicenter 84.7% of workers in micro-businesses are informal vs. only 3.1% in large firms (DANE, Q2 2024).
🪪
No RUT · No Registry In 2024, 77.6% of micro-businesses had no RUT (tax ID). In rural/populated centers that figure reaches 94% (EMICRON 2024).
🌍
Unobserved Economy (ENO) DANE estimates Colombia's informal/unobserved economy represents 24.4% of total production and 29.9% of value added (EMICRON 2024).

Phase 02 · CRISP-ML

Data Engineering

The primary dataset is the EMICRON 2023 (Encuesta de Micronegocios — DANE), publicly available at microdatos.dane.gov.co. It covers micro-businesses with up to 9 employees across 24 departments and Bogotá D.C., and is the most complete official source for mapping commercial informality at the municipal level in Colombia.

🗄️

Data Selection — EMICRON + GEIH

The EMICRON 2023 microdata (DANE) is the backbone of this project. It records over 7 modules per micro-business: economic activity (CIIU code), location, workforce characteristics, formalization variables (RUT, Chamber of Commerce), ICT access, and financial inclusion. The GEIH (Gran Encuesta Integrada de Hogares) is used as a complementary source to enrich city-level informality rates. Both datasets are open access on microdatos.dane.gov.co.

EMICRON 2023 · GEIH · datos.gov.co
🧹

Data Cleaning — Handling Real Gaps

The EMICRON has been through DANE's official anonymization protocol (removing direct and pseudo-identifiers), so no individual business can be traced. Key cleaning tasks: handle missing values in income variables (the "Ventas mes anterior" field had a 7% imputation rate in Q4 2024), standardize CIIU economic sector codes, remove out-of-scope activities (utilities, air transport, finance), and unify department/municipality naming conventions across survey years.

Preprocessing
⚙️

Transformation & Feature Engineering

From the raw EMICRON variables, we engineer a composite Informality Index per record, combining: absence of RUT (76.2% of businesses in 2023), no Chamber of Commerce registration, no ARL contributions (93.6% of owners in 2024), no accounting records, and no pension/health contributions. Each factor is binarized and weighted. Numerical variables (income, number of employees, years in operation) are normalized using StandardScaler before feeding into K-Means.

Feature Engineering
📝

Documentation & Reproducibility

All transformation steps are logged in a Jupyter Notebook hosted on GitHub, following the ML-Ops.org reproducibility guidelines. The EMICRON data dictionary (DDI/XML) is included as metadata. Each pipeline step records: data version (EMICRON 2023, survey reference Q4 2024 bulletin), Python library versions (pandas, scikit-learn), and the anonymization protocol reference from DANE's official documentation.

Phase 03 · CRISP-ML

Machine Learning Model

Unsupervised learning is the right approach here: we don't have pre-labeled "formal/informal" ground-truth labels per business, but we do have rich multi-dimensional data that reveals natural groupings. K-Means has already been applied to detect commercial patterns in Colombian cities — a 2021 study using street-view imagery in Medellín found 5 distinct clusters of commercial activity, confirming the viability of the clustering approach for this context.

Unsupervised Learning · K-Means Clustering

Detecting Informality Profiles from EMICRON Data

K-Means groups each EMICRON micro-business record into k clusters based on similarity across the engineered informality features. Each cluster surfaces a distinct trader profile — from "fully informal, no registration, cash-only" to "borderline formal, has RUT but no ARL." This segmentation lets policymakers move beyond the blunt national average (55.8%) and target specific profiles in specific cities.

🔍
Why K-Means? No labels needed. Efficient on tabular survey data. Prior research in Medellín (2021, arXiv:2104.04545) found 5 clusters of commercial activity using spatial ML — validating the approach for Colombia.
📐
Selecting k · Elbow + Silhouette We test k = 3 to 7, using the Elbow Method and Silhouette Coefficient. Expected result: 3–5 clusters mapping to clearly distinct informality profiles across the EMICRON data.
⚡
Preprocessing Pipeline StandardScaler for numerical vars (income, employees, years in operation) + OneHotEncoder for categorical vars (CIIU sector, location type, department) + PCA for 2D visualization.
📈
Evaluation Metrics Silhouette Score (target ≥ 0.4), Davies-Bouldin Index (lower = better), and within-cluster sum of squares (WCSS) for Elbow curve analysis.
🗺️
Visualization PCA 2D scatter plot colored by cluster, city-level choropleth map showing dominant cluster per municipality, and radar charts for cluster profile comparison.
🛠️
Tech Stack Python 3.11 · scikit-learn · pandas · matplotlib · seaborn · plotly · Jupyter Notebook · GitHub Pages

✅ Expected Outcome

A reproducible Python pipeline + trained K-Means model that can be applied to each new EMICRON annual release to track cluster evolution year-over-year — giving governments a live dashboard of formalization progress by city and sector.

⚠️ Known Limitations

K-Means assumes spherical clusters and is sensitive to outliers. The EMICRON's anonymization removes direct location below the department level. K must be defined a priori. Model quality depends directly on DANE's annual data release cycle.
